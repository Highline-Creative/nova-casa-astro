If you're not a UAE national or resident, you might assume owning property in Dubai is out of reach. It isn't. Since 2002, the Dubai government has allowed full foreign ownership — freehold — in designated areas across the city, and the process is more straightforward than most people expect.

Here's exactly how it works, step by step.

## 1. Confirm the area is freehold

Not every neighbourhood in Dubai allows 100% foreign ownership. Freehold areas include popular investor zones like Jumeirah Lake Towers (JLT), Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, Business Bay, Al Furjan, and Dubailand — but plenty of older, more central neighbourhoods are still leasehold-only for non-GCC nationals. Before you fall in love with a listing, confirm its freehold status. This is the first thing we check for every client.

## 2. Get your paperwork ready

You don't need a UAE visa or residency to buy. What you do need is:

- A valid passport
- Proof of funds or a mortgage pre-approval if you're financing
- A UAE bank account is helpful but not mandatory for the purchase itself

Non-residents can get a mortgage in Dubai too, though down-payment requirements are generally higher and vary by bank, property type and buyer profile.

## 3. Make an offer and sign the MOU

Once you've chosen a unit, your agent submits an offer to the seller. If accepted, both parties sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) — in Dubai this is the standard Form F contract regulated by the Dubai Land Department (DLD). At this stage, the buyer pays a deposit, usually 10% of the purchase price, held with the agency or a trustee.

## 4. Apply for the No Objection Certificate (NOC)

The seller's developer issues an NOC confirming there are no outstanding service charges or liabilities on the unit. This normally takes a few business days and both parties (or their agents) attend in person or via power of attorney.

## 5. Transfer ownership at the DLD

With the NOC in hand, buyer and seller meet at a DLD registration trustee office to complete the transfer. The buyer pays the remaining balance (usually via manager's cheque). The Dubai Land Department (DLD) transfer fee is 4% of the purchase price — by default it's generally split between buyer and seller, though in practice the buyer may agree to pay the full 4% depending on the transaction. Always confirm who is paying what before you sign.

## 6. Receive your title deed

Once the transfer is registered, the DLD issues the title deed in your name — the same legal ownership document a UAE national would receive. From this point, the property is fully yours: you can live in it, rent it out, sell it, or put it toward a UAE Golden Visa application — property investors can qualify for the 10-year Golden Visa once their investment meets the AED 2 million threshold.

## What this actually costs, all in

Beyond the purchase price, budget for:

- **DLD transfer fee:** 4% of the purchase price
- **DLD registration and administrative fees:** these vary depending on the property and transaction type
- **Agency commission:** typically 2% of the purchase price
- **NOC fee:** AED 500–5,000, set by the developer
- **Mortgage registration fee** (if financing): 0.25% of the loan amount

None of this requires you to be physically based in Dubai — with a power of attorney, the entire process can be completed remotely.

## The short version

Foreign ownership in Dubai is real, regulated, and well-trodden — thousands of international buyers complete this process every year. The steps that trip people up are usually about *which* area is freehold and *what* the all-in costs really are, not the ownership itself.
